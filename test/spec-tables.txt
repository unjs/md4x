
# Tables

MD4X always recognizes tables; the extension is unconditional.

Basic table example of a table with two columns and three lines (when not
counting the header) is as follows:

```````````````````````````````` example
| Column 1 | Column 2 |
|----------|----------|
| foo      | bar      |
| baz      | qux      |
| quux     | quuz     |
.
<table>
<thead>
<tr><th>Column 1</th><th>Column 2</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>foo</td><td>bar</td></tr>
<tr><td>baz</td><td>qux</td></tr>
<tr><td>quux</td><td>quuz</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
````````````````````````````````

The leading and succeeding pipe characters (`|`) on each line are optional:

```````````````````````````````` example
Column 1 | Column 2 |
---------|--------- |
foo      | bar      |
baz      | qux      |
quux     | quuz     |
.
<table>
<thead>
<tr><th>Column 1</th><th>Column 2</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>foo</td><td>bar</td></tr>
<tr><td>baz</td><td>qux</td></tr>
<tr><td>quux</td><td>quuz</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
````````````````````````````````

```````````````````````````````` example
| Column 1 | Column 2
|----------|---------
| foo      | bar
| baz      | qux
| quux     | quuz
.
<table>
<thead>
<tr><th>Column 1</th><th>Column 2</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>foo</td><td>bar</td></tr>
<tr><td>baz</td><td>qux</td></tr>
<tr><td>quux</td><td>quuz</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
````````````````````````````````

```````````````````````````````` example
Column 1 | Column 2
---------|---------
foo      | bar
baz      | qux
quux     | quuz
.
<table>
<thead>
<tr><th>Column 1</th><th>Column 2</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>foo</td><td>bar</td></tr>
<tr><td>baz</td><td>qux</td></tr>
<tr><td>quux</td><td>quuz</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
````````````````````````````````

However for one-column table, at least one pipe has to be used in the table
header underline, otherwise it would be parsed as a Setext title followed by
a paragraph.

```````````````````````````````` example
Column 1
--------
foo
baz
quux
.
<h2>Column 1</h2>
<p>foo
baz
quux</p>
````````````````````````````````

Leading and trailing whitespace in a table cell is ignored and the columns do
not need to be aligned.

```````````````````````````````` example
Column 1 |Column 2
---|---
foo | bar
baz| qux
quux|quuz
.
<table>
<thead>
<tr><th>Column 1</th><th>Column 2</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>foo</td><td>bar</td></tr>
<tr><td>baz</td><td>qux</td></tr>
<tr><td>quux</td><td>quuz</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
````````````````````````````````

A table CAN interrupt a paragraph: the line right above the underline is taken
as the header row, and whatever precedes it stays a paragraph. (md4c required a
blank line here and rendered the whole table as literal pipes; GitHub does not,
and a missing blank line is a common authoring slip.)

```````````````````````````````` example
Lorem ipsum dolor sit amet.
| Column 1 | Column 2
| ---------|---------
| foo      | bar
| baz      | qux
| quux     | quuz
.
<p>Lorem ipsum dolor sit amet.</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr><th>Column 1</th><th>Column 2</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>foo</td><td>bar</td></tr>
<tr><td>baz</td><td>qux</td></tr>
<tr><td>quux</td><td>quuz</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
````````````````````````````````

Only the last line of the paragraph is the header; the rest keeps its soft
breaks:

```````````````````````````````` example
one
two
Column 1 | Column 2
---------|---------
foo      | bar
.
<p>one
two</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr><th>Column 1</th><th>Column 2</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>foo</td><td>bar</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
````````````````````````````````

The interrupted paragraph is still closed the normal way, so leading link
reference definitions in it are still eaten:

```````````````````````````````` example
[ref]: /url
Column 1 | Column 2
---------|---------
foo      | bar

[ref]
.
<table>
<thead>
<tr><th>Column 1</th><th>Column 2</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>foo</td><td>bar</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p><a href="/url">ref</a></p>
````````````````````````````````

It interrupts inside a container too:

```````````````````````````````` example
> Lorem ipsum
> | Column 1 | Column 2
> | ---------|---------
> | foo      | bar
.
<blockquote>
<p>Lorem ipsum</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr><th>Column 1</th><th>Column 2</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>foo</td><td>bar</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
</blockquote>
````````````````````````````````

An underline that is only a LAZY continuation line does not, though — same rule
as everywhere else, the underline has to belong to the same container as the
header row:

```````````````````````````````` example
> Lorem ipsum
| Column 1 | Column 2
| ---------|---------
| foo      | bar
.
<blockquote>
<p>Lorem ipsum
| Column 1 | Column 2
| ---------|---------
| foo      | bar</p>
</blockquote>
````````````````````````````````

Similarly, paragraph cannot interrupt a table:

```````````````````````````````` example
Column 1 | Column 2
---------|---------
foo      | bar
baz      | qux
quux     | quuz
Lorem ipsum dolor sit amet.
.
<table>
<thead>
<tr><th>Column 1</th><th>Column 2</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>foo</td><td>bar</td></tr>
<tr><td>baz</td><td>qux</td></tr>
<tr><td>quux</td><td>quuz</td></tr>
<tr><td>Lorem ipsum dolor sit amet.</td><td></td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
````````````````````````````````

The first, the last or both the first and the last dash in each column
underline can be replaced with a colon (`:`) to request left, right or middle
alignment of the respective column:

```````````````````````````````` example
| Column 1 | Column 2 | Column 3 | Column 4 |
|----------|:---------|:--------:|---------:|
| default  | left     | center   | right    |
.
<table>
<thead>
<tr><th>Column 1</th><th align="left">Column 2</th><th align="center">Column 3</th><th align="right">Column 4</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>default</td><td align="left">left</td><td align="center">center</td><td align="right">right</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
````````````````````````````````

To include a literal pipe character in any cell, it has to be escaped.

```````````````````````````````` example
Column 1 | Column 2
---------|---------
foo      | bar
baz      | qux \| xyzzy
quux     | quuz
.
<table>
<thead>
<tr><th>Column 1</th><th>Column 2</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>foo</td><td>bar</td></tr>
<tr><td>baz</td><td>qux | xyzzy</td></tr>
<tr><td>quux</td><td>quuz</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
````````````````````````````````

Contents of each cell is parsed as an inline text which may contents any
inline Markdown spans like emphasis, strong emphasis, links etc.

```````````````````````````````` example
Column 1 | Column 2
---------|---------
*foo*    | bar
**baz**  | [qux]
quux     | [quuz](/url2)

[qux]: /url
.
<table>
<thead>
<tr><th>Column 1</th><th>Column 2</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td><em>foo</em></td><td>bar</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>baz</strong></td><td><a href="/url">qux</a></td></tr>
<tr><td>quux</td><td><a href="/url2">quuz</a></td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
````````````````````````````````

Strike-through, code spans and images work in a cell just as they do in a
paragraph; the delimiter runs and the `![` opener survive the cell-splitting
pass:

```````````````````````````````` example
| Feature  | Status     | Link                                   |
| -------- | ---------- | -------------------------------------- |
| **Bold** | *Italic*   | [Link](https://example)                |
| `Code`   | ~~Strike~~ | ![Image](https://picsum.photos/120/30) |
.
<table>
<thead>
<tr><th>Feature</th><th>Status</th><th>Link</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td><strong>Bold</strong></td><td><em>Italic</em></td><td><a href="https://example">Link</a></td></tr>
<tr><td><code>Code</code></td><td><del>Strike</del></td><td><img src="https://picsum.photos/120/30" alt="Image"></td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
````````````````````````````````

A single-tilde strike-through is recognized in a cell too:

```````````````````````````````` example
| Column 1 | Column 2 |
|----------|----------|
| ~foo~    | ~~bar~~  |
.
<table>
<thead>
<tr><th>Column 1</th><th>Column 2</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td><del>foo</del></td><td><del>bar</del></td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
````````````````````````````````

However pipes which are inside a code span are not recognized as cell
boundaries.

```````````````````````````````` example
Column 1 | Column 2
---------|---------
`foo     | bar`
baz      | qux
quux     | quuz
.
<table>
<thead>
<tr><th>Column 1</th><th>Column 2</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td><code>foo     | bar</code></td><td></td></tr>
<tr><td>baz</td><td>qux</td></tr>
<tr><td>quux</td><td>quuz</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
````````````````````````````````

This is where md4x diverges from GFM, which splits the row on every unescaped
pipe *before* inline parsing and so gets two cells here. The escape GFM
documents for that case works either way: inside a cell, `\|` is a literal pipe
even in a code span, in raw HTML and in math, where an ordinary inline backslash
escape does nothing. GFM example 200 (imported in `test/spec-gfm.txt`) pins the
code span; these are the other two verbatim contexts.

```````````````````````````````` example
Column 1 | Column 2
---------|---------
<span title="a\|b">x</span> | $c\|d$
.
<table>
<thead>
<tr><th>Column 1</th><th>Column 2</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td><span title="a|b">x</span></td><td><x-equation>c|d</x-equation></td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
````````````````````````````````

Outside a table cell the same runs keep the backslash: nothing there was ever
split on pipes, so there is no escape to undo.

```````````````````````````````` example
`a\|b` and <span title="a\|b">x</span> and $c\|d$
.
<p><code>a\|b</code> and <span title="a\|b">x</span> and <x-equation>c\|d</x-equation></p>
````````````````````````````````


### Empty cells between adjacent pipes

Two pipes with nothing between them delimit an empty cell, and a longer run of
pipes delimits several. md4c (and md4x before it) dropped every zero-width cell
instead, shifting the rest of the row left — `| a || c |` came out as `a`, `c`,
then an empty padding cell. GitHub keeps the columns where the author put them.
Imported from md4c 60965ab (`test/spec-spoilers.txt`, the no-spoilers case);
md4x has no spoilers, so every `||` inside a table row is two boundaries.

```````````````````````````````` example
| A | B | C | D | E |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a     ||c||     e |
.
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>A</th>
<th>B</th>
<th>C</th>
<th>D</th>
<th>E</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>a</td>
<td></td>
<td>c</td>
<td></td>
<td>e</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
````````````````````````````````

The row's own leading and trailing pipes are still optional, so an empty first
or last cell needs its own pipe pair:

```````````````````````````````` example
| a | b | c |
|---|---|---|
|| y | z |
| x | y ||
| x ||| 
.
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>a</th>
<th>b</th>
<th>c</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td></td>
<td>y</td>
<td>z</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>x</td>
<td>y</td>
<td></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>x</td>
<td></td>
<td></td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
````````````````````````````````

The header row follows the same rule:

```````````````````````````````` example
|| y | z |
|---|---|---|
| a | b | c |
.
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th></th>
<th>y</th>
<th>z</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>a</td>
<td>b</td>
<td>c</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
````````````````````````````````
