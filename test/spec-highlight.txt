# Highlight

MD4X always recognizes inline highlight spans, written as `==text==` and
rendered as `<mark>`; the extension is unconditional.

Ported from md4c `d2a08e5`. Note MD4X has one dialect with every extension on,
so the examples below also show how highlight composes with the other
extensions; md4c's "flag required" section cannot be expressed here.

```````````````````````````````` example
==Hello, world!==
.
<p><mark>Hello, world!</mark></p>
````````````````````````````````

Highlight spans may appear inline alongside normal text:

```````````````````````````````` example
This is ==very important== text.
.
<p>This is <mark>very important</mark> text.</p>
````````````````````````````````

Multiple independent highlight spans may appear in the same paragraph:

```````````````````````````````` example
==first== and ==second==
.
<p><mark>first</mark> and <mark>second</mark></p>
````````````````````````````````


## Delimiter length

Exactly two equals signs form a delimiter. A single one is literal:

```````````````````````````````` example
=highlight=
.
<p>=highlight=</p>
````````````````````````````````

Longer runs are not split into delimiters:

```````````````````````````````` example
===highlight===
.
<p>===highlight===</p>
````````````````````````````````

```````````````````````````````` example
====highlight====
.
<p>====highlight====</p>
````````````````````````````````

```````````````````````````````` example
=====x=====
.
<p>=====x=====</p>
````````````````````````````````

A run of four on its own is neither an opener nor a closer pair:

```````````````````````````````` example
====
.
<p>====</p>
````````````````````````````````

A lone equals sign in prose is untouched:

```````````````````````````````` example
a = b and a == b
.
<p>a = b and a == b</p>
````````````````````````````````


## Whitespace rules

Unlike emphasis, the delimiter is gated on whitespace adjacency only, not on
the flanking rules. A delimiter cannot open a span when it is followed by
whitespace:

```````````````````````````````` example
== highlight==
.
<p>== highlight==</p>
````````````````````````````````

And it cannot close a span when it is preceded by whitespace:

```````````````````````````````` example
==highlight ==
.
<p>==highlight ==</p>
````````````````````````````````

```````````````````````````````` example
== ==
.
<p>== ==</p>
````````````````````````````````

Because the rule is whitespace-based, an intra-word delimiter still works:

```````````````````````````````` example
a==b==c
.
<p>a<mark>b</mark>c</p>
````````````````````````````````


## Unmatched delimiters

An opener with no closer stays literal:

```````````````````````````````` example
==highlight
.
<p>==highlight</p>
````````````````````````````````

And so does a closer with no opener:

```````````````````````````````` example
highlight==
.
<p>highlight==</p>
````````````````````````````````

```````````````````````````````` example
a==b
.
<p>a==b</p>
````````````````````````````````

A backslash-escaped delimiter is literal text:

```````````````````````````````` example
\==x\==
.
<p>==x==</p>
````````````````````````````````


## Span boundaries

A highlight span may span a soft line break inside one paragraph:

```````````````````````````````` example
==a
b==
.
<p><mark>a
b</mark></p>
````````````````````````````````

But it cannot cross a paragraph boundary:

```````````````````````````````` example
This ==has a

new paragraph==.
.
<p>This ==has a</p>
<p>new paragraph==.</p>
````````````````````````````````

Delimiters nest, innermost pair first:

```````````````````````````````` example
==a ==b== c==
.
<p><mark>a <mark>b</mark> c</mark></p>
````````````````````````````````

A highlight span does not cross a strike-through span; the inner run is
literal:

```````````````````````````````` example
==a~~b==c~~
.
<p><mark>a~~b</mark>c~~</p>
````````````````````````````````


## Suppression inside code

Equals signs inside a code span are literal:

```````````````````````````````` example
`==code==`
.
<p><code>==code==</code></p>
````````````````````````````````

So are those in a fenced code block:

```````````````````````````````` example
```
==code==
```
.
<pre><code>==code==
</code></pre>
````````````````````````````````

And in an indented code block:

```````````````````````````````` example
    ==code==
.
<pre><code>==code==
</code></pre>
````````````````````````````````


## Nested inline spans

```````````````````````````````` example
==very *important* text==
.
<p><mark>very <em>important</em> text</mark></p>
````````````````````````````````

Emphasis nests inside a highlight span:

```````````````````````````````` example
==**bold** and _italic_==
.
<p><mark><strong>bold</strong> and <em>italic</em></mark></p>
````````````````````````````````

```````````````````````````````` example
==highlighted `code`==
.
<p><mark>highlighted <code>code</code></mark></p>
````````````````````````````````

```````````````````````````````` example
==highlighted [link](http://example.com)==
.
<p><mark>highlighted <a href="http://example.com">link</a></mark></p>
````````````````````````````````

```````````````````````````````` example
==<b>raw html</b>==
.
<p><mark><b>raw html</b></mark></p>
````````````````````````````````

```````````````````````````````` example
==&amp;==
.
<p><mark>&amp;</mark></p>
````````````````````````````````

A highlight span may itself be nested in another span:

```````````````````````````````` example
[==highlighted link==](http://example.com)
.
<p><a href="http://example.com"><mark>highlighted link</mark></a></p>
````````````````````````````````

```````````````````````````````` example
*==a==* and **==b==**
.
<p><em><mark>a</mark></em> and <strong><mark>b</mark></strong></p>
````````````````````````````````

Adjacent spans do not interfere:

```````````````````````````````` example
**==a==**==b==
.
<p><strong><mark>a</mark></strong><mark>b</mark></p>
````````````````````````````````

A permissive autolink is still recognized inside a highlight span:

```````````````````````````````` example
==www.example.com==
.
<p><mark><a href="http://www.example.com">www.example.com</a></mark></p>
````````````````````````````````


## Setext headings

`=` is also the setext underline character, but that is block-level
classification, which the inline mark collector never sees. A setext underline
is still a heading:

```````````````````````````````` example
para
===
.
<h1>para</h1>
````````````````````````````````

Including an underline of exactly two:

```````````````````````````````` example
para
==
.
<h1>para</h1>
````````````````````````````````

And a highlighted setext heading works:

```````````````````````````````` example
==h==
=====
.
<h1><mark>h</mark></h1>
````````````````````````````````

```````````````````````````````` example
# ==h==
.
<h1><mark>h</mark></h1>
````````````````````````````````


## Other MD4X extensions

Strike-through composes both ways:

```````````````````````````````` example
~~==a==~~ and ==~~b~~==
.
<p><del><mark>a</mark></del> and <mark><del>b</del></mark></p>
````````````````````````````````

Highlight inside a table cell:

```````````````````````````````` example
| Feature | Status |
| --- | --- |
| Highlight | ==done== |
.
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Feature</th>
<th>Status</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>Highlight</td>
<td><mark>done</mark></td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
````````````````````````````````

Highlight inside an inline component:

```````````````````````````````` example
:badge[==x==]
.
<p><badge><mark>x</mark></badge></p>
````````````````````````````````

LaTeX math is verbatim, so a delimiter inside it stays literal:

```````````````````````````````` example
==$x$== and $==y==$
.
<p><mark><x-equation>x</x-equation></mark> and <x-equation>==y==</x-equation></p>
````````````````````````````````

## Attributes

Unlike md4c, MD4X gives the span an attributes detail, so a trailing `{...}`
attaches to it exactly as it does to `**bold**` or `~~del~~`:

```````````````````````````````` example
==x=={.warn}
.
<p><mark class="warn">x</mark></p>
````````````````````````````````

```````````````````````````````` example
==x=={#id .a data-k="v"}
.
<p><mark id="id" data-k="v" class="a">x</mark></p>
````````````````````````````````

The braces must follow the closer immediately. With a space between, they do not attach to the
`<mark>` — they are a trailing block-attribute run and lift to the enclosing paragraph instead
(see `spec-attributes.txt`, "Block Attributes"):

```````````````````````````````` example
x ==y== {.z}
.
<p class="z">x <mark>y</mark></p>
````````````````````````````````

Braces inside the span are ordinary text:

```````````````````````````````` example
==a{.b}==
.
<p><mark>a{.b}</mark></p>
````````````````````````````````

A `==` right after the closer's `{...}` opener is not a highlight delimiter.
The run is malformed (`=` cannot start an unquoted value), so it stays literal
text — but the `==` inside it still does not open a second mark:

```````````````````````````````` example
==a=={b==c}
.
<p><mark>a</mark>{b==c}</p>
````````````````````````````````

Likewise a `==` inside another span's attribute run, exactly as before this
extension existed:

```````````````````````````````` example
**b**{data=="x"}
.
<p><strong>b</strong>{data==&quot;x&quot;}</p>
````````````````````````````````
